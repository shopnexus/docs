Since the platform judges no case, trust is a set of statistics. Nobody can tell who is right in a single case, but across fifty cases a pattern shows in the numbers.

## What is tracked

- For a buyer, the <Term id="refusal-rate">refusal rate</Term>: how often they refuse a parcel at the door, counted from the orders sellers report as returned.
- For a seller, how often they decline issue reports.
- For a seller, how many issue reports are still waiting for their answer.

The app shows each person these figures over the last 90 days.

## The ladder

A person who crosses a threshold loses rights in this order:

1. Lower visibility for their listings.
2. A lower limit on COD orders.
3. No COD at all.
4. The account is locked.

Every step is automatic and follows the figures. A sanction removes rights only, since the platform holds no money to fine anyone with. A seller plan cannot buy COD rights or a reputation tier; those come only from a person's history of deals.

The thresholds are not decided yet. They do the work a judge would do in another marketplace, so they are the next part of the model to settle.

## Open COD orders for new buyers

When a buyer refuses a parcel, the seller pays shipping both ways. The refusal rate is the only thing that keeps buyers ordering seriously, so a new buyer may have only a few COD orders waiting for delivery at once. The plan is for that limit to grow with the number of orders the buyer has received. Today the server applies a fixed limit of 3 orders requested or shipped per buyer.

## Sales outside the app

A seller who sells an item off the app marks it with **Đánh dấu đã bán**. The sale counts in the seller's sold total and in their record.

A suspended account cannot post, make offers or ask for COD. It can still finish or cancel an order already under way.
